# Presenter guidance

This guidance is separate from the website. There are no notes or timer controls on screen. Use your own pacing.

1. The problem: describe a household balancing rent, utility bills and commuting. Keep the 2023 date and below-$70,000 population qualifier visible when discussing the survey.
2. The proposal: use stations as a starting point for housing and energy research. Open the map to show real routes and LIFT projects.
3. New Utrecht: explain the documented library and housing proposal. The HPD RFP assumes rezoning. The project is proposed, not completed. The LIFT layer has a separate published unit estimate and date.
4. Existing homes: use Jefferson St to discuss preservation and improvements. The field photographs do not identify retrofit need. PLUTO shows land-use records, not building-condition surveys.
5. Shared heat: describe the opportunity to study a connection. London provides a precedent. No NYC station heat capacity or service area is claimed.
6. Station Lab: move to the separate application to examine station conditions and potential byproducts.

Use Next or the arrow keys. Explore the map can interrupt any chapter. Start with 18 Av D, open nearby property records, inspect the New Utrecht LIFT project, then compare a vacant/parking record with a residential record. The parcel source and ZoLa links support further review.

The source catalogue in `data/evidence.js` provides the direct citations used in the story.


For a short live map detour, open Housing layers. Leave LIFT and vacant/parking records on. Toggle residential points to compare the two views. Change Study distance and replay the branches. Explain that the branches are distance-based research connections, not designed TEN pipes. Select a card, then read its original source and TEN input requirements. Return to Story to continue where you left off.
